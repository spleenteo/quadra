Why Quadra

Real-world photos are never square.

You shoot a book page from an angle. A sign leans. A horizon drifts a few degrees. Fixing it is simple geometry, but the tools for it hide inside heavyweight editors, or behind websites that want your upload first.

Quadra does that one job. Drag four handles around what should be a rectangle, draw a line along what should be level, and watch the result update live.

No layers, no accounts, no cloud. Open a photo, square it up, save it.

Three tools

One goal: a square image.

Perspective

Drag four corners around any tilted rectangle, like a book page, a sign or a wall. Quadra flattens it, and the preview updates as you move.

Straighten

Draw a line along something that should be level or plumb, like the horizon. Quadra works out the rotation and applies it.

Crop

Crop with eight handles or drag the whole frame. A thirds grid helps you compose before you confirm.

And the rest

Small controls, done carefully.

Fine rotation

Slider and numeric input from -180° to +180°, with decimal precision.

Skew

Adjust horizontal and vertical skew to fine-tune perpendicularity.

Resize

Set width and height in pixels or as a percentage, with the aspect ratio locked if you like.

PNG, JPG, WebP

Save in the format you need, with adjustable quality for JPG and WebP.

Magnifier loupe

A 3× loupe follows any handle you drag, so you can place corners pixel-perfect.

Open with Quadra

Right-click an image in Finder, drag and drop it, or paste from the clipboard.

Everything happens on your Mac.

No uploads, no servers, no account. Your photos never leave your machine.
